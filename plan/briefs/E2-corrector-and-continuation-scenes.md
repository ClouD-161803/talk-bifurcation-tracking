## The two slides

Slide 8, differential correction: a periodic orbit is a zero of $\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_0, T) = \boldsymbol{\phi}(T; \boldsymbol{x}_0) - \boldsymbol{x}_0$, Newton's method linearizes it with the state transition matrix $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$, and the iterates close on the orbit while $\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$ falls quadratically. Slide 9, multiple shooting: the arc is cut at $S$ nodes, each segment must meet the next ($\boldsymbol{F}_i = \boldsymbol{\phi}(\Delta t_i; \boldsymbol{x}_i) - \boldsymbol{x}_{i+1}$), the nodes slide and the defects close, and a race over $S = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16$ shows the convergence; in the ER3BP the period is pinned at $2\pi K$, hence the Jacobian is $\boldsymbol{\Phi}(2\pi K, \boldsymbol{x}_0) - \boldsymbol{I}$, and the tabs show the ER3BP at periapsis and at apoapsis. Slide 10, continuation: freeing a parameter $p$ turns the zeros into a curve, the family; natural-parameter continuation steps $p$ and corrects $\boldsymbol{X}$, pseudo-arclength continuation steps $\Delta s$ along the tangent $\boldsymbol{\tau}_k$ of the curve $\boldsymbol{Y} = (\boldsymbol{X}, p)$ with the bordered system $\boldsymbol{G}$; both pens trace the $\mathcal{L}_1$ Lyapunov family in $C$, and on family 11 in $e$ the natural pen fails at the fold $e = 0.45386$ while the arclength pen turns.

The decisions of the plan are these.

- Each scene has two cases: the Earth–Moon $\mathcal{L}_1$ Lyapunov orbit (at $\mu = 0.012155$) as the teaching example, without the distraction of a complicated orbit, and Broucke's family 11 (at $\mu = 0.5$, its seed at $e = 0$ from `data/stability/members.json`, written by another agent tonight; if the file is not there yet, take the seed from the first row of `/Users/claudiovestini/git/tycho/code/examples/broucke/data/family_11_periapsis.csv`) as the demo on the complicated orbit.
- Scene 1 (correction): an orbit panel over a convergence panel. The clock is in value mode over $c = k + \tau$, where the iterate $k$ plays in $[k, k+1)$: the arcs grow from their nodes, the defects (red) are drawn as the gaps between the end of a segment and the next node, and the nodes slide to their next position at the end of the iterate. Earlier iterates stay as ghosts in a colormap by iteration (plasma), and the converged orbit is drawn in the Broucke style. The convergence panel plots $k$ against $\log_{10}\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$, with the five curves of $S = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16$ racing as the clock runs, and the current $S$ highlighted. Tabs: the system (CR3BP at $e = 0$; ER3BP at $e = 0.3$ with the secondary at periapsis, $\theta_0 = 0$; ER3BP at $e = 0.3$ with the secondary at apoapsis, $\theta_0 = \pi$), the targeting (a symmetric half period in the CR3BP, with free $(\dot{y}_0, T/2)$; the full period $2\pi K$ in the ER3BP, where $T$ is pinned), the segment count $S$, and the first guess (near, far, and far with a line search). Toggles: the ghosts, the defects, the solution.
- Scene 2 (continuation): a diagram panel on top, the parameter against $x_0$ (so $C$ against $x_0$ for the Lyapunov family and $e$ against $x_0$ for family 11), over two orbit panels, the natural-parameter pen on the left in orange (a horizontal predictor step then a vertical corrector path, with a red cross where it fails at the fold) and the pseudo-arclength pen on the right in blue (a tangent predictor step then a corrector path normal to it), the members colored by arclength, with tabs for the family and for the step size (small, medium, large), and the current tangent $\boldsymbol{\tau}_k$ drawn.
- The arcs of the ER3BP cases are colored by $1/(1 + e\cos\theta)$ along the arc (flat in the CR3BP), so that the pulsation is seen on the orbit itself.

## The explainer standard

The two scenes are explainers in the style of 3Blue1Brown: every element on the screen is there because the sentence beside it needs it, motion explains (the node slides because Newton moved it, the defect shrinks because the segments now meet), nothing is drawn before the viewer knows what it means, the colors mean one thing each across both scenes, and each scene opens already playing its first story so that a viewer who never touches a control still understands.
