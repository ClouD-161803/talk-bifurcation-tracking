## What the demo shows

One synchronized animation is shown on slides 11 to 13. On the left, the four in-plane Floquet multipliers $\lambda$ of one member of a family are drawn on the complex plane with the unit circle. On the right, the point $(a_1, a_2)$ of the same member is drawn in Broucke's stability diagram, with the three curves $a_2 = a_1^2/4 + 2$, $a_2 = -2a_1 - 2$, and $a_2 = 2a_1 - 2$. These curves cut the plane into the seven regions of Broucke, named after Frauenfelder and Moreno as in the field `region_name` of the data. As the family parameter runs, the multipliers move on and off the circle and the point crosses the curves. The current region is named, the path traced so far is drawn behind the point, and the crossings of $k = \pm 2$ are marked, where $k = \lambda + 1/\lambda$. Readouts give $e$, $k_1$, $k_2$, and the region.

The six tabs are the six branches, with family 11 first, since this family is the one followed in the talk. Their order is family 11 at periapsis, family 11 at apoapsis, family 7 at periapsis, family 7 at apoapsis, family 8 at periapsis, and family 8 at apoapsis. On five tabs the parameter is the eccentricity $e$, with one frame per member of the branch file. On the first tab the parameter is the arclength through the fold. Its frames are the members of `family-11-periapsis.json` from $e = 0$ to $e = 0.45$, followed by the members of `fold-family-11-periapsis.json`, which run through the fold at $e = 0.45387$ and back to $e = 0.4504$. On this tab the readout of $e$ rises and then falls. Put two marks on its scrubber and on its path in the diagram: the fold ($k_1 = 2$, orange) and the period doubling past it ($k_1 = -2$ at $e = 0.452138$, violet). Drop any member whose `symplecticity_residual` exceeds $10^{-6}$, and list the dropped members in the storyboard.

## Scales

The coefficients span four decades on families 8 and 11 ($a_2$ reaches $-50929$ on family 11 at apoapsis), while family 7 at apoapsis stays within a few units of the origin, where the regions are. Fit the ranges of the diagram per tab to the path of its branch with a margin, and draw the three curves over the whole range. Where the path spans more than two decades, scale both axes by $\operatorname{asinh}$, with ticks at $\pm 2$, $\pm 10$, $\pm 10^2$, $\pm 10^3$, and $\pm 10^4$, and say so in the axis title. Render each tab once linear and once compressed, as screenshots, and keep the choice that reads better per tab, with the reason in the storyboard. On the circle panel, keep the unit circle at radius 1. Map a multiplier of modulus $r$ to a compressed radius that leaves $\lambda$ and $1/\lambda$ mirror images across the circle, e.g. $\exp(\operatorname{sign}(\ln r)\,\ln(1 + \lvert\ln r\rvert)/2)$. Draw dashed rings where $\lvert\lambda\rvert = 10$ and $100$, and state the map once in a small legend. A multiplier of modulus 308, as on family 11 at $e = 0$, must stay inside the panel.

## The reveal steps

Map every fragment of slides 11 to 13 to the element. Slide 11 shows the circle panel alone, paused on the first frame of the first tab ($e = 0$). At its third fragment the two unit multipliers and the real pair light up, and at the fourth the readouts show $k_1$ and $k_2$. Slide 12 reveals the diagram beside the circle, with the point on the CR3BP line $a_2 = -2a_1 - 2$ at $e = 0$. At its fifth fragment the animation plays, the region names light up as the point crosses the curves, and the tabs of families 7 and 8 become selectable. Slide 13 marks the crossings: the $k = 2$ curve turns orange at its third fragment, the $k = -2$ curve turns violet at its fourth, and at its fifth the first tab plays through the fold. Each slide has its own instance of the element, reading the same JSON file, with its own initial state and reveal attributes. When you need a reveal absent from the element, add the generic feature to the element in this worktree, with a test under `node --test` and a line in the README.
