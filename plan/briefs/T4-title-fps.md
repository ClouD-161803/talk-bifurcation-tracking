## Task 1: the title trace at 120 fps on a slow machine

1. Measure first. Write a probe in `test/chrome.test.mjs` or in a new `test/perf.test.mjs`. It opens the demo deck on the title slide in headless Chrome, lets the trace run, and records the cost of each frame of the trace. This cost is the time from the start to the end of the tick, with `performance.now()` around it, or the frame time of the tracing API. Record it unthrottled and with the CPU throttled four times through the DevTools protocol (`Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`), which stands in for a slower laptop. At 120 Hz a frame has 8.3 ms in total. The budget of the drawing is therefore 4 ms per frame at the fourfold throttle, i.e. 1 ms unthrottled, at the 99th percentile over the 20 s trace, at a pixel ratio of 2. Make the test fail on the current main with the measured numbers in its message, and keep this output for the hand-over.
2. Profile before fixing. Find which part of the frame is the cost. The first suspect is the `shadowBlur` of the glow strand, which blurs on every stroke and is the usual cost on a canvas. The others are the twelve fade bands restroked on every frame, the curved paths, and the number of points of `brand/transfer.js`. Two more are a full redraw of the base canvas on every frame, where an incremental draw of the new segment would do, and the pointer tint when the pointer rests on the backdrop. Say in the hand-over what the profile showed, with numbers.
3. Fix what you found in the profile, so that the test passes without a change of the look. Possible moves are the following. The glow is drawn once into an offscreen layer and composited, or drawn as a wider stroke of low opacity under the strand rather than with `shadowBlur`. The finished part of a strand is kept on the base canvas and only the new segment is drawn per frame. The fade bands are drawn incrementally. The strand file is thinned where its points are denser than a pixel at slide size. The heads stay on their own canvas. Keep the smoothness of the curves and the colors of the records.
4. Prove that the look is unchanged. Take screenshots of the title slide at 3 s, 6 s, 12 s, and 20 s, in both modes, before and after, and compute the pixel difference of each pair. A few hundred pixels at the antialiased edges are fine, and a visible change is not. Put the pairs and the diffs under `check/audition/t4/` of your worktree.
5. Run the same measurement in Firefox through `scripts/webdriver.mjs`, since Claudio presents in Zen, a Firefox-based browser, and report the frame cost there too. If WebDriver is refused on this machine, say so, and do not install anything.
