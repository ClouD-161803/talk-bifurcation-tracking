# Storyboard of the correction and continuation scenes

In this storyboard I describe, shot by shot, the two explainer scenes of slides 11 to 14 of the bifurcation-tracking talk. The page `correction.html` is shown during slide 11 (differential correction) and slide 12 (multiple shooting), and the page `continuation.html` during slide 13 (natural-parameter continuation) and slide 14 (pseudo-arclength continuation). I build both pages with `build.py`, from the iterates of `corrector.py`, the NumPy corrector of this folder. Open a page next to this file and play it along the shots below.

## Conventions of both scenes

Every orbit is drawn in the rotating-pulsating frame $(x, y)$, in which the primary and the secondary lie at $x = -\mu$ and $x = 1 - \mu$, respectively, and the libration point $\mathcal{L}_1$ is fixed. In the CR3BP this frame is the rotating frame. The two cases of each scene are the Earth–Moon $\mathcal{L}_1$ Lyapunov orbit at the mass ratio $\mu = 0.012155$, the teaching example, and Broucke's family 11 at $\mu = 0.5$, the demo on a complicated orbit. The masses are labeled "Earth" and "Moon" in the first case, and "primary" and "secondary" in the second. Each color is given one meaning in both scenes, as shown in the table below.

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| teal | the current run of scene 1, i.e. its arcs in the CR3BP and in the ER3BP, its converged orbit, and its curve in the convergence panel |
| red | a defect, i.e. a gap that the corrector must close, a failed run, a failed attempt of a pen, which is kept until the next member, or the stop of a pen, as a larger dot |
| orange | natural-parameter continuation, i.e. the steps of its pen, its newest member, and, in dim orange on the tab "both", the members of its run |
| blue | pseudo-arclength continuation, i.e. the steps of its pen and its newest member |
| white | the current state of the corrector, i.e. the nodes and the velocity arrows, the comet of the satellite, the scale bar of the close-up of scene 1, and, faintly, the box of the close-up in the orbit panel and in the diagram |
| green | the requirement of the half period in scene 1, i.e. the target on the $x$-axis below the end of the arc and the vertical velocity required there |
| yellow | the satellite, i.e. the head that rides the converged orbit of scene 1 once it is drawn |
| violet | the tangent $\boldsymbol{\tau}_j$ of the blue pen, in the violet in which $\boldsymbol{\tau}_j$ is highlighted on slide 14, and, faintly, the constraint line across it |
| gray | context, i.e. the mirror image of a half-period arc, the $x$-axis in the close-up of scene 1, the reference solution, the ghosts of earlier iterates, the tolerance, the curves of the other segment counts and their labels, the family curve, and the earlier members of the pen that is played |

The clock of both scenes is a value clock, and one loop of it is played in 8 s at speed 1. I play scene 1 at speed 0.5, at which a loop is played in 16 s, and scene 2 at speed 0.25, at which a loop is played in 32 s. In the deck the speed of each viewer is set from its slide, and the speed menu of the transport is needed only on a page opened alone, which is opened at speed 1. Each viewer is opened paused on its first shot, and I start it with the play button.

On each slide I show the controls of the points I demonstrate on it. These are the tabs of the targeting and the first guess and the toggles of the ghosts and the solution on slide 11, the tabs of the case, the system, the segment count, and the first guess and the toggle "other segment counts" on slide 12, the tabs of the family on slide 13, and the transport alone on slide 14. Every control is kept on the pages `correction.html` and `continuation.html`, which I open in a tab for a shot that needs a control the slide does not show.

A change of a tab or a toggle does not restart the clock. To play a new run from its start, I press the key 0 right after the click on its tab, and the clock is put back to its start.

## Scene 1, `correction.html`

The page is made of an orbit panel at the left, titled "orbit", over the chip of its frame "rotating-pulsating". At the right, a close-up, titled "magnified close-up", over the chip "centered on the target", is stacked over a convergence panel, titled "convergence". Above the panels are the tabs for the case, the system, the targeting, the segment count $S$, and the first guess, and the toggles for the ghosts, the defects, the solution, and the other segment counts. In the caption, the current iterate $n$, the value of $\log_{10}\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$ for the norm of its constraint vector, and the power of ten of the scale bar of the close-up are read out. The values of an iterate are read out from the moment its point is drawn in the convergence panel, and those of iterate 0 from the start of the loop.

I cut the orbit at $S$ nodes $\boldsymbol{x}_0, \dots, \boldsymbol{x}_{S-1}$, and I require each segment to end on the next node,
$$
\boldsymbol{F}_i = \boldsymbol{\phi}(\Delta t_i; \boldsymbol{x}_i) - \boldsymbol{x}_{i+1}, \qquad i = 0, \dots, S - 1,
$$
where $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\Delta t_i; \boldsymbol{x}_i)$ is the state reached from $\boldsymbol{x}_i$ after the time of flight $\Delta t_i$ of segment $i$, and $\boldsymbol{x}_S$ is $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ over the full period. I write $\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}$ for the whole constraint vector at the Newton iterate $n$. With $S = 1$ this is single shooting, $\boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{x}_0, T) = \boldsymbol{\phi}(T; \boldsymbol{x}_0) - \boldsymbol{x}_0$. Under the symmetric half period, I instead require the last segment to reach the $x$-axis at a right angle, i.e. $y = \dot{x} = 0$ at $T/2$, and the second half of the orbit is the mirror image $y \to -y$ of the first. In the CR3BP the period is free, and the $x$ component $x_0$ of $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ is fixed. In the ER3BP the period is pinned at $2\pi K$ with $K = 1$, hence in single shooting I solve for $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ alone, and the Jacobian is $\boldsymbol{\Phi}(2\pi K; \boldsymbol{x}_0) - \boldsymbol{I}$. With $S$ segments the Jacobian is assembled from the $S$ state transition matrices $\boldsymbol{\Phi}$ of the segments. I solve the linear system of each Newton step in the least-squares sense, which gives the step of least norm when the system has more unknowns than equations, as on slide 11.

I build the first guess by sampling the converged orbit at $S$ equal steps of time over the targeted arc, and by scaling each position about the centroid of the orbit and each velocity by $1 + \varepsilon$. The guess tabs are "ε = 0.001", "ε = 0.01", "ε = 0.05", and "ε = 0.01, line search", and in the CR3BP the guessed period is $(1 + \varepsilon)T$ as well. With the line search, I halve $\alpha$ until $\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$ is reduced by the fraction $\alpha/4$, down to $\alpha = 1/64$, and otherwise I take the trial step with the smallest norm. I stop a run after ten Newton steps, when $\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$ is above 10, or when a point of an arc is within $10^{-3}$ of a body or farther than 4 from the barycenter.

The clock is labeled "iterate n + fraction", and I run it from $-2.3$. With more than one segment, every iterate $n \geq 1$ is played from $n$ to $n + 1$, in about 1.2 s at speed 0.5, in the phases listed below, and iterate 0 from the start of the loop to 1. The phases are given below as fractions of an iterate, and they hold for $S = 1$ as well. Iterate 0 is played in one of two ways. With $S = 1$, only the node and its velocity arrow are drawn on the first frame of the loop, and slide 11 is loaded paused on this still. Iterate 0 is then played from $-2.25$ to 0.35, about 3 s at speed 0.5, in the same phases at a slower pace. Its arc is drawn growing right after I press play, up to $-1.35$, its defect up to $-1.05$, and its node is moved from $-0.15$. Every later iterate of a run with $S = 1$ is played 0.65 earlier, from $n - 0.65$ to $n + 0.35$. With more than one segment, iterate 0 is staged around one segment instead. Every node is drawn white until $-2$. From $-2$ to $-1.05$, the arc and the defect of the first segment are drawn, while the other nodes are drawn dim. From $-0.95$ to $-0.1$, the other arcs and defects are drawn together, and their nodes are drawn white again. From 0.1 to 1 all the nodes are moved at once. With more than one segment, the point of iterate 0 is drawn in the convergence panel from the first frame of the loop, and with $S = 1$ in its phase 3.

1. From $n$ to $n + 0.45$, the teal arcs of iterate $n$ are drawn growing from their white nodes. With $S = 1$, a white arrow is drawn at the node for its velocity, and with more segments each node is drawn as a white dot without an arrow. At the median speed of the reference orbit, an arrow is 7% as long as the longer side of the box of its case. Its head is 9 CSS pixels long at any zoom. Under the half period, the mirror image of the arcs is drawn growing in gray at the same time, since it is obtained by reflection rather than integrated. An arc that leaves the orbit panel is cut at the box to which the panel is fitted, i.e. near the edge of the panel, and its pieces inside the box are drawn.
2. From $n + 0.45$ to $n + 0.6$, a red defect is drawn growing at the end of each arc, i.e. the gap in position from the end to its target. Its target is the next node, or under the half period the point $(x, 0)$ on the axis below the end, with the vertical velocity $(0, \dot{y})$, since $\boldsymbol{F} = (y, \dot{x})$. The gap of the half period is thus always vertical. A red disc of 12 CSS pixels is drawn at the target, under the white dot of a node. A gap shorter than a pixel is thus still seen at 730 by 880 pixels, as a red ring around its node or as a red dot. With $S = 1$, a white arrow is drawn at the end of the arc for its velocity, and under the half period a green one at the target for the velocity required there, since neither is a node. The two arrows are thus seen as a V, which closes on a periodic orbit, where the white arrow lies on the green one. An arrow is drawn only while it lies inside the panel. The gap of an arc that ends outside the panel is drawn from the point where the arc was cut.
3. At $n + 0.6$, the point $(n, \log_{10}\|\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\|)$ is added to the teal curve of the current $S$ in the convergence panel, and the caption is updated to iterate $n$ at the same time.
4. At $n + 0.7$, the arrow of the node is removed, while the arrows at the end of the arc are held until $n + 1$. From $n + 0.7$ to $n + 1$, the nodes are moved to their positions in iterate $n + 1$, which is the Newton step seen in the orbit panel. The gaps in position and their discs are held until $n + 1$. The arcs and the defects of iterate $n$ are then replaced by its ghost, i.e. a thin gray arc, which is drawn faintly under the current arcs while the toggle "ghosts" is on.
5. When $\|\boldsymbol{F}^{(n)}\|$ is below the tolerance $10^{-11}$, the arcs of iterate $n$ and their arrows are kept to the end of the loop, without defects. The two arrows at the end of the arc are then seen as one vertical arrow, the white one over the green one. From $n + 0.8$ the converged orbit is drawn still, in teal over a teal glow, its nodes are removed, and a satellite laps it to the end of the loop, once every 1.5 of the clock, about 1.7 s at speed 0.5, as a yellow head with a short white comet. After a run fails at the iterate $K$, its last arcs are kept, and with $S = 1$ the arrow of its node as well, since its node is not moved again. A red cross is then drawn in the convergence panel at $n = K$, at the last finite value of $\log_{10}\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$. The label "run failed at n = K" is drawn in the text color beside a red dot, at the top of the panel and right of the cross, or further left where it would leave the panel. The caption then reads out $K$ and this last finite value.

In the close-up, the end of the last arc is magnified about its target, i.e. the target on the axis under the half period and the node $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ over the full period. The target is held at the middle of the close-up, as a green dot on the gray axis or as the white node. While an iterate is held, the close-up is drawn at the scale of its gap, at which the end is drawn 0.6 of the half height of the close-up from the target, with the gap as a red line and the last part of the arc in teal. From $n$ to $n + 0.35$, the end is moved to its position in iterate $n$, and the arc of iterate $n$ is drawn growing onto it. When the new gap is smaller, the end is moved at the old scale, hence the gap is seen to collapse onto the target, and from $n + 0.45$ to $n + 0.85$ the close-up is zoomed in about the target to the scale of the new gap, often by several powers of ten. When the new gap is larger, the close-up is zoomed out while the end is moved, hence the end stays in view, and the red gap is drawn only afterward, at the new scale. I draw the zoom as a flipbook of 14 copies, one per frame of the clock, since a TyCHO camera is fitted only once. The area shown in the close-up is outlined in the orbit panel by a faint dashed white box, at least 12 px wide. The scale bar at the lower left is $10^p$ long, at the largest power of ten $p$ for which the bar is at most the half height of the close-up, and $p$ is read out in the caption. The converged iterate keeps the scale of the iterate before it, hence its end is seen to land on its target. The close-up shows the gap in position only, and not the defect in velocity, hence it can be zoomed in further than $\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$ is reduced. With more than one segment, it shows the closure at $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ alone, which is one of the $S$ defects.

In the convergence panel, $\log_{10}\|\boldsymbol{F}\|$ is plotted against the iterate $n$ under a free aspect, with the axis titles "iterate n" and "log₁₀ ‖F‖" and a gray line at the tolerance. The curve of the current $S$ is drawn in teal with a white head, which is moved from one computed point to the next as each is added, and is never drawn between two of them. With the toggle "other segment counts", the curves of the four other values of $S$ for the same case, system, targeting, and guess are drawn in gray, and their failures are marked by faint red crosses. The five curves are thus drawn side by side as a race. Each gray curve is labeled at its last point with its value of $S$, e.g. "S = 4", in the gray of the curve. The teal curve of the current $S$ is labeled in the same way in teal, e.g. "S = 16", and each of the five curves of a race is thus named. I draw each label as strokes, since a TyCHO label is always drawn in the text color. I place the labels of a race one at a time, beside the last point of the curve, or above or below it where it is a cross. Each label is kept clear of the curves, the crosses, the tolerance line, the label of a failed run, and the labels placed before it. The toggle is off and not shown on slide 11, so that the curve of $S = 1$ is seen alone, and I tick it on slide 12 when I name the race.

The main line of slide 12 is shot 3, with the race of the segment counts, and shot 5, in which I click the ER3BP at fragments 3 and 4. Shots 4 and 6 are material for questions, which I play on slide 12 with its own controls, and shot 7 needs the toggles of the ghosts and the defects, hence I play it on the page `correction.html` opened in a tab.

## Scene 2, `continuation.html`

The page is made of three panels. At the top left is the diagram, in which a coordinate of each orbit is plotted against the parameter $p$ of the family. For the Lyapunov family I plot $x_0$, the abscissa at which the orbit crosses the $x$-axis at a right angle, against the Jacobi constant $C$. This italic $x_0$ is the $x$ component of the initial state, the bold $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ of scene 1. For family 11 I plot the launch velocity $\dot{y}_0$ at this crossing against the eccentricity $e$. At the top right, over 60% of the width of the row, is a close-up of the diagram, whose box is outlined in faint white dashes in the diagram. On family 11 the close-up is centered on the tip of the fold, at $e = 0.453865$ and $\dot{y}_0 = 1.1411$, and spans $e$ from 0.4510 to 0.4568 and $\dot{y}_0$ from 1.1394 to 1.1428. On the Lyapunov family it is fitted to the first failed step of the orange pen at the medium step, from $C = 2.956$ to 3.027. The box is drawn at least 12 CSS pixels on a side, so that it is seen on family 11, where the close-up spans less than a pixel of the diagram. The orbit panel, titled "members, rotating-pulsating frame", spans the bottom row. The label of $\mathcal{L}_1$ is drawn below the members, at the end of a thin gray leader from $\mathcal{L}_1$, so that it is not hidden under the orbits. On family 11, where $\mathcal{L}_1$ lies at $x = 0$, the leader is drawn along the $y$-axis. In the orbit panel, the earlier members of the pen that is played are drawn in dim gray, and its newest member in the color of the pen. The seed of the pen, i.e. its first member, is drawn from the start of the loop, hence the orbit panel is not empty while a slide is paused there.

Above the panels are the tabs for the family, for the step size (small, medium, and large), and for the pens. With the tab "natural parameter", the default, the orange pen is played alone. With the tab "both", the blue pen is played alone over a finished record of the orange pen, which is drawn from the start of the loop. In this record, the members of the orange pen are drawn as dim orange dots, and the record is ended at its last member, without a dot at its stop. Its predictors, its failed attempts, and its corrections are not drawn. The small red dots of the failed attempts of the blue pen are thus the only red at the fold. In the caption, the family, the parameter of the moving pen to four decimals, and the number of halvings of its step from the full step of the tab are read out. The halvings are updated at each new member, and they are raised at each attempt that follows a failed one. The moving pen is the orange pen on the first tab of the pens and the blue pen on the second.

When I free the parameter, the isolated zeros of $\boldsymbol{F}$ become a curve of zeros $\boldsymbol{Y} = (\boldsymbol{X}, p)$, the family, which is drawn in gray in the diagram. I compute this curve past the reach of both pens. I draw it only up to the last member of the farthest pen played on the tab, so that a pen at its end is not seen to stop short of the curve. A pen stopped below the minimum step is left out. Where every pen played is stopped, as on family 11 with the tab "natural parameter", the curve is drawn up to the farthest pen at the step size. In natural-parameter continuation I step $p$ by $\Delta p$, keep $\boldsymbol{X}$ as the predictor, and correct $\boldsymbol{X}$ at the new $p$. In pseudo-arclength continuation I step a distance $\Delta s$ along the unit tangent $\boldsymbol{\tau}_j$ of the curve at the last member $\boldsymbol{Y}_j$, and I correct on the hyperplane normal to the tangent, i.e. I solve the bordered system
$$
\boldsymbol{G}(\boldsymbol{Y}) = \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol{F}(\boldsymbol{Y}) \\ \boldsymbol{\tau}_j^T \boldsymbol{W} (\boldsymbol{Y} - \boldsymbol{Y}_j) - \Delta s \end{bmatrix} = \boldsymbol{0},
$$
where $j$ is the index of the member, $\boldsymbol{W}$ is a diagonal weight matrix, and $\boldsymbol{\tau}_j$ is a unit vector in the norm of $\boldsymbol{W}$. On family 11, I scale $\dot{y}_0$ and $e$ each by its range over the family, and I give $x_0$ a weight of zero. On the Lyapunov family, I scale $x_0$ and $C$ each by its range, and I give $\dot{y}_0$ and the half period a weight of zero. The norm of $\boldsymbol{W}$ is thus the length in the diagram, with each axis scaled by its range. I draw the diagram and the close-up so that a unit of this norm is as long across the panel as up it. Hence the tangent is drawn as a unit vector, and the constraint line at a right angle to it. I scale both coordinates by their full ranges, rather than $e$ by a fraction of its range, so that the curve fills the diagram.
